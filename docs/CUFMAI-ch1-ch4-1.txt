Chapter 1

THE PROBLEM AND ITS SCOPE

Introduction          Shoe industry is an essential aspect of economic activities, especially regarding the development of retail business in the local community. Shoe business helps provide jobs, contribute to the local economy, and offer the local population the opportunity to purchase goods that are practical, comfortable, and fashionable. Shoe retail business aims to provide the local population with the best possible products, such as affordable, high-quality, fashionable, and customized designs, according to the customer’s demands. As the demands of the local population continue to change, it is expected that the shoe retail business will develop better products and services to compete with other business organizations in the market. However, the shoe retail business still uses traditional business operations, which cannot handle the demands of the local population, such as product comparison, customer queries, and customization. Using technology can improve the efficiency of the shoe retail business, especially regarding customer engagement (Dwivedi et al., 2020).

          With the widespread use of smartphones, retail companies can now incorporate technological solutions that can improve their operations and customer engagement (Kemp, 2024). Footwear retail systems can utilize smartphone-based technologies such as virtual fitting assistance, allowing customers to try footwear using their smartphone cameras (An et al., 2021; Chou et al., 2024). Studies on augmented reality (AR) and virtual try-on (VTO) applications show that virtual try-on experiences can enhance customer engagement and influence purchase intentions by improving product evaluation (Abassi et al., 2025; Mollel & Chen, 2025). Retail systems may also include digital customization features where customers can select design options, such as color schemes and design variations, and preview their preferred designs before purchasing. By incorporating a centralized database, these systems can store and manage product information, inventory records, customer data, and purchase history to support efficient retail operations.          Although the relevance of local shoe businesses cannot be overlooked, there are certain common problems that limit their efficiency and customer satisfaction. Manual entry of sales and traditional inventory management may result in incorrect stock data, delayed entries, and confusion regarding which products are sold faster or slower. On the customer side, choosing the appropriate size and style can be difficult without fitting, especially for customers inquiring online or buying directly, resulting in size mismatches, dissatisfaction, or returns. Additionally, design requirements may become unorganized, especially when customer preference data, status, and completion dates are not being tracked (Immadisetty, 2025).

          To address these issues, the researchers propose OTOP: Footwear Retail Information System with Virtual Fitting Support, a system designed to modernize footwear retail operations while improving customer experience. OTOP will offer the customers centralized inventory management, sales tracking, customer record management, and reporting capabilities that will help in effective decision-making and quick services. The virtual fitting functionality of OTOP will enable the customers to view footwear on their virtual body using a smartphone, thus increasing their confidence levels in choosing appropriate products and minimizing errors in fitting. Moreover, the customers will be able to design footwear options using the customization module of OTOP (Abassi et al., 2025; Chou et al., 2024).

Project Context

          OTOP is created in collaboration with a local footwear retail business in Carcar (Valladolid area), Philippines. The business targets walk-in clients from the surrounding neighborhood who purchase footwear for both daily and special events. Like other local footwear businesses, the business operates in a fast-paced retail environment where clients frequently inquire about sizes, designs, prices, and availability, and where the business has to act quickly to keep sales and satisfy clients.

	This system will be used by the store personnel and the customers who interact with the store during the purchase activities. Through this system, the store personnel will be able to manage the store’s inventory records, sales transactions, customer information, and sales reports through a centralized system. The customers will be able to view the designs of the footwear available for purchase, the customization of the footwear, and the virtual fitting through a smartphone application.

The study is set to begin in February 2026 and is expected to be completed by December 2026. The project will be implemented in the Business Processing and Licensing Office in the City of Naga.

The researchers are proposing the system to solve the inefficiencies involved with the operation of retail stores and to aid the digitalization of small retail stores. With the inclusion of inventory management, recording of transactions, virtual fitting, and product customization within the proposed system, the researchers are aiming to improve the overall operation of the retail store and the shopping experience of the customer.

The research will adopt the methodology of Extreme Programming (XP). XP is an agile software development approach. This approach is characterized by iterative software development, feedback, and close interaction between software developers and users of the computer systems (Beck, 2004). This approach has been identified as effective in ensuring flexibility and user adaptability to changing software requirements while ensuring software quality standards (Pressman & Maxim, 2020). Through the XP approach, the researchers hope to create a reliable and user-friendly software that can meet the needs of the footwear retail business operation.

Purpose and Description

          The main purpose of OTOP: A Footwear Retail Management System with Virtual Fitting Support is to create a virtual system that will improve the retail activities of the footwear store. The project aims to resolve the limitations associated with the conventional store activities such as the recording of sales, monitoring of the store's inventory, and management of customized footwear. The conventional activities of the store make it hard for the store personnel to manage the store's inventory. The project aims to improve the activities of the store through the creation of a virtual system that will improve the store's activities.

	The objective of the project is to design a retail information system for the footwear retail store, which includes inventory control, recording of transactions, customer information recording, and report generation through a database system. The proposed information system for the retail store will enable the store staff to keep track of inventory, record transactions, and manage customer information systematically. Additionally, the information system will provide the option of using smartphones to provide virtual fitting to the customer, enabling the customer to see the look and feel of the footwear before making a purchase. Further, the information system will provide the option of customizing the design of the product to the customer and express the design preferences clearly to the store staff.

The following individuals will benefit from the project:

Customers. Benefit from faster product browsing, simplified checks for available sizes, and virtual fittings using your smartphone to increase size confidence and reduce returns. Customers looking for personalized footwear will find the customization option particularly useful.

Store Owner/Management. Enhanced operations through centralized inventory, organized sales data, inventory notifications, and analysis for informed re-stocking and pricing, while minimizing losses due to inaccuracies and forgone sales.            

Footwear Makers. Receives clearer and more organized custom order details (design, size, and due date), helping streamline production and reduce misunderstandings.

Researchers. Develops practical skills in building a real-world retail information system with mobile virtual fitting, including system design, database management, and usability evaluation.

Future Researchers. Can use the project as a reference for future studies related to retail systems, inventory management, virtual fitting, and customization technologies.

Objectives

          The main objective of the study is to design and develop OTOP: Footwear Retail Management System with Virtual Fitting Support that can improve the operational workflow of a local footwear shop by centralizing inventory management, sales monitoring, and customer order tracking while enhancing customer experience through smartphone-based virtual fitting and customization support.

More specifically, this capstone project aims:

• To conduct a preliminary investigation to gather relevant and necessary data regarding the current retail operations of the partner footwear store.

• To analyze the gathered data in order to determine the functional and system requirements needed for the development of the proposed OTOPsystem.



• To design and develop the OTOP system that manages product records, inventory levels, footwear sizes, sales transactions, customer information, and reports through a centralized database, while integrating smartphone-based virtual fitting and customization features.

• To test and evaluate the developed system using the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) 25010 software quality characteristics and the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) for system acceptability.

• To deploy the fully functional system to the partner footwear retail stores.

Scope and Limitations

          The scope of this project focuses on improving the retail operations of the partner footwear shop through a centralized system that automates and manages retail store operations such as inventory monitoring, sales processing, order handling, customer transactions, and customization request. The system also covers customer-facing features such as product browsing, size checking, virtual fitting preview, and customization request submission. Data gathering will focus on store processes, user requirements, and operational issues relevant to footwear retail activities.

          However, the study includes limitations. The virtual fitting feature will provide a visual preview and size support but does not guarantee perfect fitting accuracy due to factors such as camera angle, lighting conditions, and device limitations. The customization module is limited to store-approved design options and available materials. External factors such as internet connectivity, device performance, and changes in store policies are beyond the control of the system and are not covered in the scope of the study (An et al., 2021; Chou et al., 2024).

Retail Operations Management 

Retail operations management is essential for ensuring the smooth and efficient functioning of retail businesses, especially for small and medium enterprises (SMEs). It involves managing daily store activities such as inventory control, sales processing, customer service, and order handling. Efficient retail operations help businesses improve productivity and provide better customer experiences. However, relying on manual processes can lead to delays, errors, and poor coordination within the store. Studies show that businesses that implement structured retail operations management practices achieve better performance and operational efficiency (Berman & Evans, 2021).

Effective inventory management is one of the most important aspects of retail operations. When inventory is properly monitored, businesses can avoid stock shortages and overstocking, which directly affect sales and profitability. Research indicates that real-time inventory tracking systems allow store owners to make faster and more accurate decisions, improving overall business performance. This also helps ensure that products are available when customers need them, leading to higher customer satisfaction (Waller & Fawcett, 2013).

In addition, retail operations management focuses on improving workflow efficiency and reducing operational costs. The use of automated systems can streamline store processes such as sales transactions, order processing, and stock updates. Studies show that automation reduces human error and increases the speed of operations, allowing businesses to serve customers more effectively. Point of Sales (POS) Systems

Point-of-Sale (POS) systems play a crucial role in modern retail businesses by facilitating sales transactions and managing store operations. A POS system combines hardware and software to process payments, record sales, and update inventory in real time. Studies show that implementing POS systems significantly enhances transaction speed and reduces errors in retail environments (Raymond & Uwizeyemungu, 2007).

Modern POS systems offer more than just transaction processing. They include features such as inventory management, customer data tracking, and sales reporting, which help businesses make informed decisions. By linking sales data with inventory systems, retailers can monitor product demand and adjust their stock levels accordingly. Research indicates that businesses using POS systems can better respond to customer needs and improve overall service quality (Pantano & Viassone, 2015).

Furthermore, POS systems contribute to better financial management and business analysis. They generate detailed reports on sales performance, allowing store owners to identify trends and evaluate business growth. These insights help retailers develop effective strategies and improve profitability. With the integration of advanced technologies, such as cloud-based systems and mobile POS, retail businesses can operate more flexibly and efficiently, making POS systems a vital component of retail management systems (Sharma & Aggarwal, 2019).

ISO/IEC 25010 Software Quality Model

The ISO/IEC 25010 software quality model is a popular software quality model that is used as a standard software quality model and has characteristics such as functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, usability, reliability, security, maintainability, and portability. Functional suitability is used to measure whether the system meets the user’s requirements, while performance efficiency is used to measure the response time and resource consumption. Compatibility is used to measure the system’s ability to function with other systems, while usability is used to measure the ease of learning and using the system. In addition, reliability is used to measure the stability of the system, security is used to measure the protection of the system, while maintainability is used to measure the ease of updating the system. Portability is used to measure the adaptability of the system.

The latest research also employs the ISO/IEC 25010 standard in measuring software quality. For example, Ahmad et al. (2023) applied the ISO/IEC 25010 standard in measuring the quality of a mobile-based learning system, emphasizing the importance of usability and performance efficiency in measuring software quality. Pratama and Mutiara (2021) also applied the ISO/IEC 25010 quality model in measuring the software quality of the Halodoc mobile application, ensuring that the standard is effective in measuring the functional and usability aspects of real software.

The ISO/IEC 25010 model can be particularly utilized to evaluate the quality of OTOP since it covers all the aspects of functionality, usability, reliability, security, and performance efficiency. Since the system combines inventory management and customization features, the use of the ISO model would ensure an objective evaluation of the system.

Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT)

	The Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) is a widely adopted theory for explaining the acceptance of technology. This theory was introduced as a means of identifying the factors that influence an individual’s intention to use a system, as well as the actual use of the system. The UTAUT model is a combination of other technology acceptance theories, emphasizing the importance of the determinants that influence the adoption of information systems by users. This includes performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, facilitating conditions, which explain the behavior of users regarding the adoption of technological innovations (Venkatesh et al., 2003).

	Performance Expectancy. Performance Expectancy is defined as “the extent to which users perceive that using the particular system will improve their performance in performing the task.” In the proposed OTOP system, store staff and users may find the system useful if it will help them to efficiently manage the store’s inventory, monitor sales, obtain product details, and facilitate virtual fitting.

	Effort expectancy. Effort expectancy relates to the level of ease associated with the use of the system. In this study, it relates to the level of ease associated with the learning of the OTOP system by the store owner and the customers. A system with a clear interface, ease of navigation, and ease of function will always be accepted by the user.

	Social influence. Social influence measures the degree to which users perceive that important people in their surroundings believe they should use the new system. In the particular setting of this study, sources of social influence could be the customers, store staff, and other people who recognize the importance of digital retail systems in improving store operations.

	Facilitating conditions. Facilitating conditions are the extent to which users perceive that the organization and technology infrastructure exists to support the system use. This includes the presence of smartphones, internet connection, devices, and support for the user in accessing the information system for the sole purpose of shopping, as well as the virtual fitting room.

From the literature reviewed above, it can be noted that most literature emphasizes the significance of digital technologies in enhancing retail operations and customer experience. Some of the common ideas noted in the literature include retail automation technologies, retail inventory and sales management systems, mobile commerce technologies, augmented reality technologies, and user-friendly system design technologies. These technologies assist businesses in improving their operations and managing their inventory more effectively, thus improving the overall customer experience. In terms of functionalities, retail systems offer businesses different functionalities such as product information management, sales management, inventory management, and customer interaction technologies. Advanced retail technologies offer businesses functionalities such as virtual fitting and product customization technologies, thus enabling customers to visualize products and customize their products before making a purchasing decision. In addition, mobile-based retail technologies assist businesses in reaching their customers more conveniently using their smartphones, thus improving their relationship with customers.

However, despite all these developments, some gaps exist that need to be addressed with regards to the current studies and systems. Some of the gaps include the fact that most of the current retail technologies available are mainly suited to larger businesses or brands that can afford the technological requirements. There is a lack of integrated systems that can offer inventory management, virtual fitting, product customization, and mobile retail under a single umbrella that can cater to the needs of small or local footwear retailers. In addition, there is a lack of studies that can offer an evaluation of the integrated systems using software quality models to ensure the usability and dependability of the systems. This shows that there is a need to develop an integrated retail information system that can cater to the needs of small footwear businesses or retailers. This can be achieved by integrating retail automation technology, virtual fitting technology, product customization technology, and evaluating the system to ensure usability.

Development Methodology

As shown in Figure 3, the study employed using the Extreme Programming Methodology also known as XP Methodology. Extreme Programming is an agile software development framework that advocates short development period, continuous testing and produces high software quality. Extreme programming is highly suitable for making short-term project, such as this study, that only involves a couple of months after deploying the system. The continuous detection of bugs, issues, and rapid response to the changing requirements because of the continuous deployment and testing delivers a higher software quality and enhanced user experience. Requirements Analysis

            The requirements analysis includes an understanding of the system and its description, analysis, and prioritization. It is crucial to understand the needs and expectations of the users so that they can be satisfied with the developed system. The study seeks to address the inefficiencies experienced in traditional footwear retail operations through the development of a system called OTOP, which includes aspects of inventory management, sales recording, customer information handling, virtual fittings, and customization. By identifying the challenges facing traditional footwear retail operations, such as inaccurate inventory records, wrong sizes, and unorganized customization, the researchers have been able to identify the functional and non-functional requirements of the system. This is crucial in ensuring that the developed system not only addresses inefficiencies in traditional operations but also satisfies customer needs for a competitive edge in the market.

            Population and Locale of the Study. This part of the requirements analysis section describes the population and locale of the study. The population refers to the group of people who are actively engaged in the process of footwear retail. This includes the owners of the store, who personally manage the store as an all-around store personnel, and the customers who frequently buy footwear and are actively engaged with the system using the browse, virtual fit, and customization tools. These people play an important role in the study to evaluate the effectiveness of the OTOP system in fulfilling the needs of the users and how it can be further improved. The locale of the study is based on the description of the environment where the process of footwear retail is actively engaged. This locale was chosen because it is an accurate representation of small and medium-sized enterprises in the footwear industry that are having problems with inventory management, customer engagement, and customization, especially when they are compared with larger retail brands. The study is being done in this locale to provide an accurate testing environment for the OTOP system.

                        Population. In this section, we find the population of the study. The population includes those individuals who are directly engaged in activities related to footwear retailing. This includes store owners who directly manage their stores through all-around personnel, those who handle sales, inventory, and customer records, and also includes customers who regularly purchase footwear and use the system through activities such as browsing, virtual fitting, and customization tools. This variety of roles and activities helps in ensuring that the system is reviewed from different angles, and the feedback and experiences obtained from them are important in determining how OTOP can enhance its usability, efficiency, and satisfaction in terms of footwear retailing activities.	

                        Locale of the Study. This section will describe the location of the study. The location of the study is in Valladolid, Carcar City, Cebu, an area known for its thriving retail business in footwears and shoemaking. By conducting the study in this location, the researchers are able to see the practical application of OTOP in the day-to-day operations of the retail business. This location is significant because it will provide an actual situation to test the effectiveness of the OTOP system.
