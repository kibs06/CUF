**Created:** September 27, 2026
**Updated:** October 5, 2026 — **The AR camera's black screen was one dropped call, and the way it was dropped is the finding: the screen's only `startAr` came from the platform view's creation, and the model is never staged that early.** The capture (20:09–21:06, 157 `NavDiag` lines) holds no `setDiagnostics`, no `startSession` and no `session resumed (startSession)`; the platform's camera was opened **once in the whole window — by the foot scan** (`ArFootSizingView`, 20:11:43.7) — while the AR view sat mounted for **48 minutes** (`presented=173,660`) drawing its clear colour. Every mount in that window was AR-mode, not the preview page (`camera feed: EGL context prepared and shared with the engine` is printed only under `Mode.AR`), and the reason they read like previews is that the AR channel's `setModel` is the one handover that relays nothing — the parked spec is replayed silently at view creation, so `load: model -2` appears with no `setModel` line anywhere in `nav_diag`. **The race:** `ar_fitting_screen.dart` called `startAr(arViewReady: true)` exactly once, from `onPlatformViewCreated`, while `TryOnSessionController.startAr` returns at its `_phase != TryOnPhase.modelReady` gate — on a phone the view is created a frame or two after the screen builds and the handover (resolve → verify → hand over) lands later, so *view first, modelReady later* is the usual ordering; the early return leaves `_arStarted` false, and nothing ever called again. `needsArStart` (`phase == modelReady`) had existed since V3 for precisely this and was never wired. **The fix is that seam, wired:** the screen records the view's creation (`_tryOnViewCreated`) and both call sites — `onPlatformViewCreated` and the controller's notification path — funnel into `_startArIfReady()`, which retries while the controller reports `needsArStart` and never passes `arViewReady: true` before the view provably exists (a guessed `true` is F17's 15 s parking timeout and the `arViewNotReady` fallback, i.e. it would turn the race into the simulated screen). The dropped call now logs `[TryOn] startAr dropped: phase=…`, because its silence is what let a black camera live for 48 minutes. **A second native crash surfaced in the same window and is not explained:** 21:00:19.86, `SIGSEGV/SEGV_MAPERR, fault addr 0x4` inside `libfilament-jni.so` (`Renderer_nRender+24`) ← `Renderer.render` ← `ArTryOnView.renderFrame` on the `solevision-tryo` thread, 1.3 s after a completed model load with `session == null` (so neither the AR camera branch nor the preview branch of `renderFrame` had run); the tombstone is kept (`build/qa/crash_renderloop_21h00.log`) and the protocol's §3.1 records it as the next capture's watch item. **Desk-verified:** analyzer clean over `lib`/`test` (the four errors `flutter analyze` reports project-wide are all in generated `build/models/shade.dart`, untouched), **2 new widget tests** in `ar_fitting_try_on_swap_test.dart` — a model that lands *after* the view starts the session (the test fails on the old code with `Actual: []`, which is the regression this phase exists for) and a staging model that never starts a camera — full suite **2,466 pass / 7 skipped**; the APK rebuilt, re-signed with the release key, installed as an update, and the retry confirmed present in the installed `kernel_blob.bin` (`_startArIfReady`, `startAr dropped`). **Not proven, and it is the next minute of device time:** that the retry starts a session on the phone and the camera draws — §3.1 lists the four log markers that answer it, and every V4.9 threshold still waits behind them.
**Prev. update:** October 5, 2026 — **the first real device session found the crash no desk could reach: the AR load was dying inside Filament's own `setTransform`, because the transform write handed it an entity where a component instance belongs.** With the phone connected (Redmi 24094RAD4G, Android 16, QA build 1.0.45), the first Try On in AR load printed `load: entities added — applying the transform` and the process was gone: `Fatal signal 11 (SIGSEGV), code 1 (SEGV_MAPERR)`, whose backtrace bottoms out in `Java_com_google_android_filament_TransformManager_nSetTransform` with `ArTryOnView.applyTransform` ← `applyPendingModel` directly above it — the same window the P30 Pro used to "stall" in, now named, and not catchable (`runCatching` cannot see a native fault, which is why the log simply stopped). The cause is a handle mix-up Filament makes unusually easy: the engine hands out two different packed `int`s — the **entity** (`FilamentAsset.getRoot()`, the handle the view kept) and the **component instance** — and the two APIs split them (`hasComponent` / `create` / `getInstance` take the entity; `setTransform` takes the instance); a wrong-space handle compiles, then faults in native on the first real load. `SceneView`'s own `Node` resolves `getInstance(entity)` before every write, and the view now does the same: `modelRootTransformInstance()` (create-if-missing behind `hasComponent`, re-read on every write because the component store is packed and compacts when any component is destroyed — every asset swap destroys one) plus a single `writeModelTransform()` used by both the load transform and the tracker's per-frame transform, so the two sites cannot disagree again. The load now relays the pair (`load: root transform — entity=… instance=…`), and the probe line (`load: transform written — reading the bounding box`) prints only after a write actually returned — a refused write is recorded and logged once per load instead. **Desk-verified:** Kotlin compile clean, **44/44 JVM tests**, the QA APK rebuilt, signed with the release key and installed as an update, and the fix confirmed inside the very `classes20.dex` its own crash report names. **Not proven, and it is the next minute of device time:** whether the load now completes — the re-run on the fixed build is the evidence, and every V4.9 threshold still waits on it.
**Prev. update:** October 5, 2026 — **V4.9's desk half is built, and its first finding was about V4.9 itself: the device session could not have read its own numbers, because the V4 heartbeat had no way to be switched on for the try-on session.** Every V4 phase wrote its evidence into `ArTryOnView.emitStatus`'s one-line readout — `foot=`, `len=`, `scale=`, `mask=`, `thermal=` — for a phone with no `adb logcat`; but the only call that turns that readout on (`setDiagnostics`) lived on the **preview** channel, and the try-on session's own channel had no equivalent, so on a device `diagnosticsEnabled` stayed false and `emitStatus` returned at its first line. The fix is the seam end to end: the try-on channel now has `setDiagnostics` (Dart wrapper + plugin case with the same F18 parking the preview uses), the controller gained the testable `diagnostics` parameter (`AppConstants.shoePreviewDiagnosticsEnabled` at the screen's construction site; default `false`, so no existing caller changes behaviour or makes a call), `TryOnStatusEvent` parses the heartbeat into a new `nativeStatusLine` `ValueNotifier`, and the screen draws it as one dim line under the top panel — the preview's readout, restyled for a camera. **The other half of the evidence is the relay:** `footLock` edges and the session start (`session resumed (startSession)`) now go through `DiagRelay`, so the locked-down test phone — whose developer options are password-locked, so `adb logcat` will never be read from it — carries its own lock-rate evidence in the exported `nav_diag` file. **The session itself is now a command:** `qa-apk.yml` gained the try-on inputs (`try_on`, `foot_track`, `qa_model`, all default off so the box artifact is unchanged), and `tool/qa_capture.sh --tryon` samples the heartbeat *and* `dumpsys meminfo` every 5 s and computes the exit criterion's own number (`tryon_sessions`, `tryon_locks_within_10s`, first-lock seconds per session) from the two log markers, on logcat's clock. **The protocol is written** — `docs/RoadMap/VIRTUAL_FITTING_V4_9_DEVICE_REVIEW.md`: P1–P10 with thresholds (the ≥85% lock rate, the five-reviewer occlusion blind review, backgrounding recovery, twenty cycles ≤ 250 MB, thermal, length-vs-tape, framing, fallbacks, verdict sanity), recording sheets, and the closing rule that a failure becomes V4.10's reproduction rather than a re-run. **And the first on-device finding arrived the same day, with the phone actually connected:** the kit made the *session* reachable but not the *entry*. The product page's 3D gate (`resolveShoePreview`) asks the prefetch for a **live model row**, so while `product_models` holds 0 rows it answered `noModel` on every product — and the "Try On in AR" button lives inside the viewer that gate hides, so a QA build with every try-on define on still had no way in. The V3.5 boundary substitution now reaches the page's prefetch too (`placeholderModelService()` behind the same QA switch, only where the bytes come from), pinned at the call site. **Desk-verified:** analyzer clean, **10 new Dart tests** (3 channel: the call's payload, a `status` line verbatim, an empty one; 4 loop: the readout asked for before the session, no call without the switch, the line reaching the notifier, a failed request not costing the session; 1 screen: the line drawn, and absent without one; 2 source pins: the screen's construction site still hands the readout switch to the controller, and the page's prefetch still takes the QA model seam) — full suite **2,464 pass / 7 skipped**; Kotlin compile clean (`--rerun-tasks`, zero `tryon/` warnings), `assembleDebug` successful, **44 JVM tests**. **Not proven, and it is the whole phase:** no part of the protocol has been executed — the seam is compile-checked, `--tryon` has parsed a synthetic log and never a capture, and every threshold waits on the 10 real products and real feet V4.9's row has always named.
**Prev. update:** October 5, 2026 — **V4.8: the try-on session now survives the app — backgrounding is a pause both halves undo, twenty open/close cycles leave nothing armed, and the screen pins itself to portrait while a session is live.** The perf/stability pass had four items, and three of them turned out to have real defects underneath rather than measurements to take. **Backgrounding was the largest:** leaving the foreground destroys the platform view's surface, which pauses the ARCore session — and nothing resumed it, so a customer who took a call came back to a session that stayed paused for the rest of the visit (`startAr` is one-shot per screen open). `ArTryOnView` now records *why* a session paused (`pausedForSurfaceDetach`: the surface's own pause, never an explicit QA `pauseSession` or a torn-down view) and undoes exactly that pause when `surfaceCreated` returns — dropping the cached detection frame at the same time, so the resumed Dart loop finds a skipped tick instead of a frozen frame from before the backgrounding. The Dart half is `TryOnSessionController.setAppForeground`, called by the screen's new `WidgetsBindingObserver`: every non-`resumed` state pauses the 200 ms loop (a backgrounded screen buys no frames and runs no ML Kit), and only a real resume restarts it — **and only if this pause is what stopped it**: a loop stopped by five consecutive failures, a session that degraded while backgrounded, and a disposed controller all stay stopped, because none of those is a backgrounded app. A pause is not a stop: it leaves `liveFoot` alone (the session is not over) and moves no phase. **Twenty open/close cycles** exposed two un-cancellable `Future.delayed` timers (the 2.5 s simulated lock and the 1.8 s product relock) and a listener the screen never removed from an *injected* controller; both timers are `Timer`s cancelled in `dispose`, the listener comes off first either way, and the tests hold the line — twenty closings under either deadline fail the binding's own pending-timer check if a regression returns one, and a spy controller counts `addListener`/`removeListener` balanced across twenty mounts. **Rotation lock** is the screen's: `portraitUp` in `initState`, `DeviceOrientation.values` restored in `dispose`, so no other route inherits the lock and a session cannot be rotated into the one layout nobody has run on a device. **Thermal** is the one item a desk cannot execute, so it got the desk half: the QA heartbeat now carries `thermal=` (`ThermalReport` maps `PowerManager.getCurrentThermalStatus`, pure and JVM-tested; `n/a` below Android 10, `unknown(n)` for a value it has no word for), and the numbers — the read on a throttling phone, session memory across the cycles, real backgrounding recovery — are V4.9's device session. **Desk-verified:** analyzer clean, **13 new Dart tests** — 7 loop (`try_on_foot_track_test.dart`: pause/resume, the inert pause, the failure stop a resume must not undo, the degraded session it must not start, the reading a pause keeps, the post-dispose call, and twenty cycles with `fakeAsync`'s timer ledger asserted empty) and 6 screen (`ar_fitting_lifecycle_test.dart`: the lock and its restore, the lifecycle order forwarded to the controller, the inert no-session case, the simulated timer across twenty cycles, the listener balance, the product-switch relock) — full suite **2,454 pass / 7 skipped**; Kotlin compile clean (`--rerun-tasks`, zero `tryon/` warnings), `assembleDebug` successful, **44 JVM tests** (41 + 3 for `ThermalReport`). **Not proven:** everything the phone has to say — that a backgrounded session really comes back (the native resume is compile-checked, never run), what a throttling phone's thermal word reads, and the ≤250 MB no-leak budget across the cycles.
**Prev. update:** October 4, 2026 — **V4.7: the saved profile is compared against the foot in frame, and the comparison can only suggest — never switch.** The try-on screen's verdict grades the live foot (V4.6); this phase adds the one card that talks about the customer's *saved* scan, and it is a study in not overstepping. **The rule** (`lib/utils/try_on_saved_size.dart`, new, pure): a locked reading and a saved length that differ by **more than 8 mm** (`kStaleSavedFootMm`, the roadmap's number, strictly greater — exactly 8 mm is a foot measured twice on different days) produce a notice; everything else is silence — no lock, no reading, a broken or implausible number on either side (the engine's own 110–360 mm band), or a difference inside it. **Both sides are already the app's own numbers:** the live one is V4.6's eased `TryOnLiveFoot.lengthMm` (the number the drawn shoe is scaled with), and the saved one is `FootMeasurement.maxFootLength` — the number `footFitInputFrom` grades the verdict with, so the comparison cannot drift from the recommendation it is questioning. **It never touches the size:** the notice carries two millimetres and no size at all, so no caller *can* switch one from it; the card's only action is a route to the scan flow (`FootInstructionsScreen`). **The card** (`lib/widgets/try_on/try_on_saved_size_card.dart`, new) draws `GlassTone.warning` glass with the roadmap's own sentence — "Your saved size may be stale — rescan?" — plus both millimetres and "Your size won't change unless you rescan."; it reads the saved scan with the V1 card's one-read-per-mount rule (the provider holds a measurement only after a scan *in this session*), so a returning customer is not silently without a profile, and a failed read draws nothing rather than a suggestion the app cannot back. It is the first child of the try-on screen's bottom column, above V4.6's verdict — the only card that moves when it appears, so the two cards the customer is acting on keep their positions over the bottom panel. **No wire change, no native change, no switch:** the comparison consumes only what V4.6 already publishes. **Tests:** 18 new — 9 pure rules (`try_on_saved_size_test.dart`: every silence, both directions, the exact-8 mm boundary), 7 widget (`try_on_saved_size_card_test.dart`: nothing without a scan, the sentence and both numbers, the lock gate, the button opening the scan flow, one read per mount, 320 px/1.6×) and 2 screen (`ar_fitting_try_on_swap_test.dart`: the notice above the verdict when the profile disagrees, silent when it agrees) — analyzer clean, **2,441 pass / 7 skipped**. **Not proven:** whether the live and saved numbers are comparable on a real foot (left/right asymmetry, socks, foreshortening) and whether 8 mm is the right band are V4.9's device session; the card has never been drawn over a live camera.
**Prev. update:** October 4, 2026 — **V4.6: the fit verdict now stands beside the shoe, graded from the live measurement — and the phase's honest core is what had to cross the wire to make that true.** V4.3's measured length existed natively and deliberately never crossed the channel; V4.6 adds exactly one event for it — `footMeasure {lengthMm, quality}`, emitted at 2 Hz (500 ms throttle) whenever the tracker has an anchor, carrying the **eased** length (the same number V4.3 sizes the drawn shoe with, read through a new pure `FootPoseTracker.measure()`) and the smoothed quality. The new pure rules (`lib/utils/try_on_fit.dart`) turn the product row, the selected size and that one reading into one of three states: **hidden** (no last spec, no gradeable size, no lock, no measurement yet, or a number the engine refuses), **the verdict** (the engine's answer when it is confident), or **the nudge** — the roadmap's own "Scan a bit closer for a better verdict" — when the reading is real but not convincing. **Four decisions are the phase.** *(1) Live means live:* the card grades the foot in the frame, never the saved scan — V4.7 is where the two get compared, which is not the same as merging them. *(2) The lock gates speech:* the shoe keeps drawing through an unlock (V4.2's rule), but a claim about fit does not survive the tracker losing the foot. *(3) The engine's own confidence floor decides which card* (`FitVerdict.isConfident`, the same 0.45 the product page honors) — so a low-quality lock gets an ask instead of a hint printed as an answer, and the trigger is the composite confidence (quality × length-only completeness × extrapolation), not a raw quality threshold: a good lock seven sizes from the measured sample is nudged too. *(4) Length only, and it says so:* the tracker measures heel→toe and nothing else, so `widthMm` stays null and the engine's own reasons name the gap rather than pretending it was checked. Controller: `liveFoot`, a `ValueNotifier<TryOnLiveFoot>` taking the measurement from `footMeasure` and the lock half from each `footLock` edge, cleared whenever the loop stops; a non-finite or non-positive length is **refused** rather than clamped into a verdict, while quality is merely clamped (a confidence, not a measurement). The card (`lib/widgets/try_on/try_on_fit_card.dart`, new) stacks above V4.5's coach card in one column, reuses the V1 card's phrase/icon/colour functions so two surfaces cannot phrase one band two ways, and `GlassTone.error` joins the shared glass for the two wrong bands. **Verification:** Kotlin `:app:compileDebugKotlin` clean, `:app:assembleDebug` successful, **41 JVM tests** green (21 tracker incl. the four new, 11 mesh, 9 correction); analyzer clean, **28 new Dart tests** (12 rules, 6 widget, 2 contract, 6 loop, 2 screen) — full suite **2,423 pass / 7 skipped**. **Not proven:** whether the live length is *right* — the toe UV landing, foreshortening, an occluded toe — is exactly what V4.9's device session answers, and the 2 Hz throttle and the confidence floor are the numbers it tunes; the card has never been drawn over a live camera. **The flag is still off; nothing has run on hardware; V4.7 is next.**
**Prev. update:** October 4, 2026 — **V4.5: the session now says what to do, and the saying is a pure function of state it already had — one rules table, one card, no new wire.** `lib/utils/try_on_coach.dart` (new, pure Dart) owns the whole judgement: `TryOnCoachCue` (ten cues), `TryOnFootFraming` read from the detection's own heel→toe span (`kFootFramingTooFar` 0.30 / `kFootFramingTooClose` 0.92, **both inclusive** — a foot exactly at a threshold is frameable, not far; workshop numbers in the detector's normalized space, V4.9's to retune), `kFootSceneHintAfter` 2 s and `kFootManualOfferAfter` 15 s, a **total** `footFramingFromDetection` (missing/degenerate/non-finite points → `unknown`, which the rule treats as no opinion rather than a reason to speak), and one `coachCueFor` whose priority is what a customer can act on, top to bottom: **a lock** (above everything, even a pending offer), **the offer** — the loop stopped after five failures (`!trackingActive`) or 15 s passed without a lock; dismissed it becomes "tap the floor" and is not re-made until the next lock, which also clears the dismissal so a later loss can offer again — **a loss** (regain), **the framing** from the very detection that fed the pose (too far → move closer, too close → move back, else hold still; framing outranks stillness because a still foot a metre away still will not lock), and **nothing seen** (point at the foot for 2 s, then floor + light — deliberately one sentence, because the app cannot tell those two failures apart). **Two implementation decisions are the phase.** *(1) The clock is counted ticks, not wall time:* `kFootTrackInterval × _footSearchTicks`, incremented only while no lock is held and reset by a lock — the loop's own cadence is what "15 s of trying" means, it stops when the loop stops, and it is exact under `fakeAsync`. *(2) The cue rides a `ValueNotifier`, not `notifyListeners`:* it changes on beats the session's other listeners do not care about, and a screen rebuilding five times a second to redraw a sentence that changes a handful of times per session is the wrong coupling; the value is assigned only when it actually changes. **Nothing new crosses the wire and no new switch exists** — every input (tracking active, phase, lock, the last detection, the counted clock) was already in the controller. The card itself (`lib/widgets/try_on/try_on_coach_card.dart`, new) is the scan's own `GlassCard` (the architecture planned this widget as "same visual language as the scan's coach card"), tone per cue (locked success, regain warning, hold-still/offer active), a **Place it myself** button **only** for the offer — its callback is `dismissManualOffer`, and the tap that follows is V3.4's native path, so nothing here calls a channel — and `SizedBox.shrink()` for a null cue, which is every build with `tryOnFootTrackEnabled` off. The screen places it above the bottom panel behind the switch. **Verification:** analyzer clean, **29 new tests** — 15 rules (`test/utils/try_on_coach_test.dart`: totality, the inclusive thresholds and clock, each priority pair as its own test), 7 widget (`test/widgets/try_on_coach_card_test.dart`: a sentence per cue, tones, the button's callback, place-by-tap having none, every cue at 320 px/1.6× text), 7 loop (`test/providers/try_on_foot_track_test.dart`: the cue rides lock→loss, the 15 s offer→dismiss→place-by-tap→re-offer arc) — full suite **2,395 pass / 7 skipped** (from 2,366). One test-helper float bug was found and fixed rather than papered over: constructing a span as `0.2 + span − 0.2` rounds 0.92 up to 0.9200000000000002, so the helper is zero-based and the boundary case tests the rule rather than the arithmetic. **Not proven, and all of it is one device session:** whether the card's placement reads well over a live camera, whether the 0.30/0.92 bands match a real foot's framing, and whether a customer who reads "hold still" actually locks is V4.9's. **The flag is still off and nothing has run on hardware; V4.6 is next.**
**Prev. update:** October 4, 2026 — **V4.4: the foot now reads as inside the shoe — the mask crosses the channel, becomes geometry, and is painted over the shoe with the camera's own pixels.** Three new pieces and one honest deviation from the row's own wording. *(1) The Dart half:* the detector's segmentation field is box-averaged to 32×32 (`lib/utils/foot_mask.dart`, 1 KB per accepted frame) and attached to the `FootDetectionResult` it was extracted from — **deliberately unclipped by the guide box**, because a capture-time box has no business deleting a toe from an occlusion — then sent beside the pose it belongs to, in its own try/catch with no failure counter (a mask that does not cross costs one frame of occlusion, never the tracking loop; a null mask is the pose detector's legal answer). *(2) The geometry:* `FootMaskMesh.kt` (new, pure Kotlin) thresholds through the sign bit (a Kotlin `Byte` of 200 is −56), dilates by one cell, and upsamples ×2 by taking a bilinear sample of the binary field at each sub-cell centre — one lit cell becomes exactly 36 sub-cells, and a mask touching the frame border does not fall off it. *(3) The draw:* `FootMaskOverlay.kt` (new) builds **its own instance of the feed's vendored `camera_stream_flat.filamat`** (independent lifecycles, the same parameter/attribute verification `ArCameraFeed.attach` does against the live engine), uploads per accepted frame into worst-case-sized buffers narrowed with `setGeometryAt` (no Filament object built per frame), and maps every quad corner through **`uprightImageUvToPixels` — the very function `hitTestFloorPoint` casts the heel and toe rays through** — a shared function precisely so the occlusion cannot sit somewhere the pose did not. **The deviation, stated plainly: this repaints rather than stencils.** Filament 1.72.1 does expose per-instance stencil state, but a stencil route needs a stencil-capable swap chain (a change to how this view's surface is created — unverifiable from a desk, black screen as the failure mode) *and* a stencil test on the shoe's glTF-owned material instances. Painting the camera's own pixels back over the shoe ends in the same pixels with neither: same texture, same shader, same frame, by construction — and its worst case is *no occlusion*, not no shoe. The shoe yields to it under two independent guarantees: the mask draws at priority 0 (the shoe is pushed to 1, the feed sits at 7 — Filament draws higher priorities first) **and** the mask instance clears depth testing while writing near depth, so a shoe drawn afterwards fails the test inside the mask. `mask=` joins the QA heartbeat, saying which of "never attached", "no mask yet" or "stale" happened. **Verification:** **11 new `FootMaskMesh` tests** (37 JVM tests total: 11 mesh, 17 tracker, 9 correction), Kotlin `:app:compileDebugKotlin` **clean** (`--rerun-tasks`, zero `tryon/` warnings), `:app:assembleDebug` **successful**, analyzer clean, **2,366 pass / 7 skipped** (the +14 is exactly the 11 mask tests and 3 loop tests). **Not proven:** anything a camera would settle — whether the mask is the right *shape* on a real foot, whether the occlusion reads as "foot inside shoe" (V4.9's blind review), and whether a device's draw ordering behaves as the priority rule intends.
**Prev. update:** October 4, 2026 — **V4.3: the shoe is now sized by the foot in the frame, and the ×N is a pure function with a JVM test instead of a per-frame guess.** The rule is a ratio of lasts, mirrored from the fit engine rather than invented: `N = (measured foot + 11 mm toe room) / the last the selected size grades to` — 11 mm is the midpoint of §2.6's true-to-size 8–14 mm band, and the denominator is the same 6.67 mm/EU grade `setSize` already applies. `FootScaleCorrection.kt` (new, pure Kotlin) owns the formula, the 0.70–1.30 clamp and the refusals (×1 — the size chart, untouched — for a missing anchor, a non-finite input, or a foot/last outside the engine's plausible bands: 110–360 mm foot, 120–360 mm last). The measurement rides the tracker: `Sample.lengthMeters` is the floor-plane heel→toe distance `sampleFoot` already computed for its degeneracy check, one-euro-filtered (a seventh instance), eased onto `Anchor.lengthMeters` with the anchor's own 60 ms τ, and rejected as a non-sample when non-finite or non-positive. `ArTryOnView` keeps the graded last (`renderedLastMm`, cleared on a model swap) and multiplies through one `effectiveScale()` shared by the load transform and the tracker's per-frame transform — the same reasoning that already keeps `authoredLengthCorrection` in one place. **This row's “relaxed-pass measurement vs live scalars” resolves toward the live scalar:** the saved measurement is what the selected size already encodes, and the row's own example (a 200 mm foot must render a shoe sized for 200 mm, not 265) is exactly what the correction does. **No wire change:** the scalar is derived natively, so the Dart half and §2.8's contract are untouched. ## 0. How to read this roadmap

- **Fit ships before render.** The fastest real value (a trustworthy size verdict on the product page) needs no 3D assets at all, only millimetres the app already has. The 3D try-on follows on top of it.
- **Each phase is independently shippable and independently revertable** (the house rule from `SIZE_AWARE_SHOPPING_PLAN.md`). Visible phases ship behind a const feature flag; P0-style foundations are inert until the flag is on.
- **The long pole is not code — it is 3D assets.** Engineering is ~8–11 weeks; producing 20–50 sellable shoe models is a parallel content programme that starts in week 1, not after the code lands.
- **Effort units:** Small < 3 days · Medium 3–7 days · Large 1.5–3 weeks · Very Large 3+ weeks.
- **Gate before every phase close:** `flutter analyze lib test && flutter test` passes, plus the phase's own exit criteria.

> **V2.6 is in code (2026-09-28), and it is the write path's other half.** The read service has its first caller: the product page warms the cache for the selection it opens on. Three things are worth reading the code for. *(1) Why a new class instead of a call to `ensureLocal`:* `ShoeModelService` **throws** on an integrity mismatch, which is the right contract for a renderer that can fall back to the simulated screen and the wrong one for a download nobody asked for — `TryOnPrefetch` is the policy that converts every failure into a result, so a warm cache can never cost the page it was warming. *(2) Why the page does not await it:* a prefetch that could delay the first frame would be a worse version of the problem it exists to solve, and the future it ignores cannot complete with an error, so `.ignore()` hides nothing. *(3) Why only the initial selection:* re-warming on every size or colour tap would download a model per tap, and whether the rendered model even depends on the selection is a question only V3 can answer (§2.7.2's `variant_id` is per variant; in practice it is product-level today).
>
> **One test-harness boundary this phase found, worth writing down for V3.** `testWidgets` runs in a **fake-async zone**: `tester.pump` advances a fake clock, and a real `dart:io` future — `File.exists()`, the verified `.glb` write — never completes there. Every path through `ensureLocal` starts with such a call, so the download stage cannot be driven to completion from a widget test; and opening `tester.runAsync` to allow it also lets the *unrelated* runtime font fetch run and fail (`google_fonts` downloads its families and they are not bundled, so neither `allowRuntimeFetching` setting avoids a thrown error). The page tests therefore prove what the page can be given — resolution failures and an unavailable cache — and the download, integrity and cache-hit paths stay pinned in the service tests with a temp directory and real I/O. V3's renderer tests will hit the same wall for the same reason; the seam that avoids it is injecting the file work, not reaching for `runAsync`.
>
> **V2.2/V2.3 are in code (2026-09-28) — and the phase can now be fed.** Until today the pipeline had a validator, a table, a bucket and a read service, but no way to get a file *in*: every V2 task that mattered ended at a partner holding a `.glb` that nobody could upload. `add_edit_product_screen.dart` now carries a "3D Model (Optional)" section that takes the link and the declaration, fetches the bytes, runs the contract, and publishes on Save. The design points worth reading the code for:
>
> 1. **A link, not a picker — and that is a gap, not a preference.** `image_picker` cannot select a `.glb` and no general file picker is a dependency of this project (verified: `file_picker` is not even transitive), so the handover arrives as a URL the studio shares and `DioShoeModelBytesSource` fetches it — with the byte cap enforced **mid-download** (`CancelToken`), because a wrong link (a Drive preview page) can be arbitrarily large and buffering it whole would be a memory failure on the cheapest phones in this market. The link must be https, except loopback so a developer can stage a file locally. Adding a picker later is a second source feeding the same `ShoeModelAsset`.
> 2. **Bytes before the row, always.** A `product_models` row whose object is missing renders as "no model" at best and as a failed download on every launch at worst, while an object with no row is simply invisible — the same asymmetry V2.5's `.part`-then-rename write protects on the read side, so the upload writes storage first and the table second (and a test asserts that order).
> 3. **Re-publishing a file is a no-op.** The digest is the storage filename, so `shoeModelReuseMatch` finds the existing row and flips its status instead of writing a duplicate — without that rule, "publish this model again" would create a second row pointing at one object and give the resolver two candidates for one asset.
> 4. **A failed check is a report, not an exception.** The seller has to read *which* rows failed, so the check returns a report either way; only a transport problem (bad scheme, 404, private link, unreachable host) throws, and each cause has its own sentence.
> 5. **A model failure never rolls back the product.** The upload runs after the product row is saved and reports itself in the snackbar ("Product saved, but the 3D model did not upload…") — losing the seller's product to a bad asset would be the D8 mistake in reverse.
>
> **Three deferrals, each because a required input does not exist yet.** *(a) The file picker* — no dependency can select a `.glb`, so the link is what ships. *(b) The per-colour override* (`variant_id`) — the app's variants are colour **and** size (`{'color': 'Black', 'size': 'EU 42'}`), so attaching a model to one variant id would show it on exactly one size: the resolver's exact-match rule would faithfully honour a row that means something the seller never intended. A per-colour model needs either a colour column or a resolution rule by colour, and that is its own decision (D-2). *(c) The material map* — `product_models.material_map` stores `{part: {baseColorHex, metallic, roughness}}`, but the product's colours are free-text **names** with no paint value (`product_color_images` carries only a photo URL), and the repo's single name→colour map (`lib/utils/variant_swatch_color.dart`) is a **UI swatch** whose unknown-name branch is a deterministic *synthetic* fallback. Adopting it as albedo would paint a shoe a colour nobody chose — the same mistake the fit card refuses to make by hiding a verdict it cannot support. The column, `ShoeModelSpec.materialMap` and the validator's approved-part check all stay ready for a real paint value.
>
> **Why the section was off, and why it now ships on (2026-09-28).** `shoeModelUploadEnabled` used to default to `false`, with `--dart-define=SHOE_MODEL_UPLOAD=true` to opt in, on the reasoning that V2.1's table and bucket were unwritten: a shipped build's Upload button could then only ever produce "relation does not exist", and a broken button is worse than an absent section. **That reasoning was sound and its premise was false** — the table had already been applied and never recorded, so the switch was waiting on nothing. It now defaults to `true`; `--dart-define=SHOE_MODEL_UPLOAD=false` restores exactly the previous day's form, and the kill switch is read by the save path as well as the section, so turning it off cannot leave a half-published model. **The caveat that outlives the flip:** the pipeline is now *reachable*, not *fed* — `product_models` holds 0 rows until a partner hands over a real `.glb`, so the section is live for every seller and there is nothing to show it yet.

**Exit criteria:** 3 **real pairs** have been captured with their partners, cleaned, and have validated active models that download and cache on device — declaration sheet and validator output attached to each; a deliberately broken GLB is rejected both client- and server-side; cache eviction proven under the 50 MB budget.
**Rollback:** assets are additive; disable by leaving `status='draft'`.

> **V4 status (2026-10-04, later the same day) — the publish-and-track path exists and now sizes itself by what it measures; what remains is everything only a phone can settle.** V4.1's Dart loop publishes at 5 Hz behind `AppConstants.tryOnFootTrackEnabled` (**off**, separate from V3's switch on purpose, and pinned by a source test because a `bool.fromEnvironment` cannot be flipped under `flutter test`); V4.2's native side answers it — a 150 ms NV21 frame cache, two floor hit tests per observation, a filtered world anchor, and `footLock` edges emitted from the tracker that owns the hysteresis; V4.3 closes the loop's most visible failure mode by sizing the shoe to the measured foot (a 200 mm foot must not wear a shoe graded for 265 mm), in a pure function with refusals and a clamp rather than a raw ratio. **The deliberate choices to re-read on a device:** a rejected frame is a missed sample, never an error (the loop's five-failure stop is the only failure policy); the lock is native-only, so Dart and the renderer cannot disagree about it; the tracker keeps drawing — and keeps its measured length — even when the lock drops, because a shoe that detaches or resizes at 0.44 is worse than one drawn from a slightly stale foot; and the frame-scale correction is clamped to 0.70–1.30 because the measurement is a second opinion, not an oracle. **V4.4 landed the same day, and it is the phase that makes the shoe read as *worn* rather than *stuck on*:** the mask crosses the channel (32×32, unclipped by the guide box, sent beside the pose in its own try/catch), becomes a dilated, bilinearly-upsampled quad list pinned to the sub-cell by JVM tests, and is drawn over the shoe as a second instance of the feed's own camera material — **repainting the foot's pixels rather than stencilling them**, which is what §2.10's outcome asks for and needs neither a stencil-capable swap chain nor a stencil test on glTF's shoe materials. Two independent guarantees make the shoe yield: priority (mask 0, shoe 1, feed 7 — higher draws first) and a near-depth write with culling off, which works under either ordering. The quads are mapped by the *same* function the heel and toe rays are cast through, so the occlusion cannot drift from the anchor. `mask=` joins the QA heartbeat with the three answers a bug report needs (never attached / no mask yet / stale). **What this does not make true:** no pose has crossed the channel on hardware (no device run, no lock-rate number, and the occlusion has only ever been arithmetic in a JVM test), whether a UV toe point lands on the real toe is unmeasured (V4.9), `product_models` still holds 0 rows, the flag is a development seam and must never reach a customer build, and V4.8–V4.9 are unwritten — so the exit criteria below remain unmeasurable, exactly as V3's did. **V4.5 landed the same day, and it is the phase that gives the session a voice:** one pure rules table (`lib/utils/try_on_coach.dart`) turns state the loop already held — the phase, the native lock, the last detection's own heel/toe points, a counted-tick clock — into ten cues with a stated priority (**a lock** above everything; the 15 s manual offer above "bring your foot back"; framing above "hold still"), and the scan's own `GlassCard` says them. Live means live — the verdict grades the foot in the frame, never the saved scan (V4.7 compares the two), and the tracker's length-only nature is stated by the engine's own reasons rather than hidden. **What V4.6 does not settle — whether the live length is right at all (toe UV, foreshortening, occlusion), and whether the 2 Hz throttle and 0.45 floor are the right numbers — is the same device session's work.** **V4.7 landed next, and it is the phase that questions the saved profile without touching it:** a pure comparison (`lib/utils/try_on_saved_size.dart`) against the same numbers the app already trusts — the locked live length on one side, `maxFootLength` on the other — says "your saved size may be stale — rescan?" only when they differ by more than 8 mm, the notice carries no size (so "never silently switch sizes" is a property of the types), and the card reads the saved scan once per mount and draws nothing when it cannot back the suggestion. **What V4.7 does not settle — whether the live and saved numbers are comparable on a real foot (left/right asymmetry, socks, foreshortening) and whether 8 mm is the right band — is the same device session's work.** **V4.8 landed last, and it is the phase where "perf and stability" turned out to mean repairs: three of its four items had a defect under them.** The largest was **backgrounding**: the platform view's surface dies when the app leaves the foreground, `surfaceDestroyed` pauses the ARCore session, and nothing ever resumed it — the customer returned to a session frozen for the rest of the visit, because Dart's `startAr` is one-shot per screen open.
