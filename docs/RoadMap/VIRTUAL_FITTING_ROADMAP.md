**Updated:** September 28, 2026 — **A real partner asset arrived, and the contract's first red rows were fixed rather than filed: V2.7 gains a normaliser.** The file is a marketplace/AI-generated shoe — 31.5 MiB, 49,954 triangles, three 4096² PBR maps, one material called `Material.001` — and it failed **7 of the 11 checks** on exactly the mechanical grounds the guide's §C1–C2 warn about: the scene's Z-up→Y-up rotation was left **on the node** (invisible to a validator that reads accessor bounds, and to the server), the length ran along **X** instead of Z, the shoe stood **4.30× life size** (1160.8 mm long, i.e. 270 mm of shoe), the origin sat at the middle of the shoe, the textures were 4096², and the single material cannot be colour-swapped. All of it is repairable arithmetic, so `lib/utils/glb_normalizer.dart` (**16 tests**) + `tool/prepare_shoe_model.dart` now do it: bake the node transform into the vertices, yaw the length onto +Z (with `--toe` naming the end that is the toe, because a bounding box is symmetric about that question), scale to `--external-length-mm` (never invented — without the declaration it re-axes and re-grounds but deliberately leaves the scale alone, so the scale row keeps failing on purpose), ground/centre to heel-bottom-centre, re-bake textures to ≤1024² (4096² → 1024² JPEG turns 30 MiB of maps into 418 KB), rename and, where the file has only one material, cut a `sole` band geometrically with its area share reported. It **refuses** the two things that cannot be repaired offline — Draco/meshopt geometry (nothing can decompress it here) and KTX2 textures — instead of writing a mangled file. **Result on the real asset: 31.48 MiB → 1.66 MiB, L×W×H 270.0 × 111.7 × 100.7 mm, 11/11 PASS.** Two things the exercise proved that no fixture had: the pipeline's contract is reachable from a *marketplace* file (good news for V2.8/V2.9 sourcing), and the asset is a **shell** — 578 disconnected surface islands, 11,408 open edges — which is the guide's §6 "the model is a lookalike, not the product" row and stays a reviewer decision, not a validator one. Analyzer clean; **2,027 pass / 6 skipped** (20 new); `dart run tool/validate_glb.dart` green on the produced file, and the placeholder asset still passes all 11 after a normalise pass (round-trip test). **And the file is now reachable on a device: the V3.5 QA seam serves it.** `assets/models/qa_partner_shoe_v1.glb` (1.66 MiB) is bundled **for the seam that is off by default**, and `--dart-define=TRY_ON_QA_MODEL=assets/models/qa_partner_shoe_v1.glb` makes the production `ShoeModelService` hand *it* to the renderer instead of the 29,340 B block-out. The reason is specific: the block-out is 1,536 triangles of untextured block geometry, so it cannot tell a renderer that decodes three 1024² PBR maps from one that draws grey clay — which is the failure a mid-range phone is most likely to show. The seam now carries per-asset row numbers (a 49,954-triangle asset reporting the block-out's 1,536 would make the QA comparison meaningless), an unknown `TRY_ON_QA_MODEL` **falls back to the block-out and can be reported as a typo** rather than silently serving the wrong file, and four new tests hold the line — including one that runs the **authoring contract over the bundled bytes**, so the seam cannot carry a file that stopped conforming after the day it was produced

## 0. How to read this roadmap

- **Fit ships before render.** The fastest real value (a trustworthy size verdict on the product page) needs no 3D assets at all, only millimetres the app already has. The 3D try-on follows on top of it.
- **Each phase is independently shippable and independently revertable** (the house rule from `SIZE_AWARE_SHOPPING_PLAN.md`). Visible phases ship behind a const feature flag; P0-style foundations are inert until the flag is on.
- **The long pole is not code — it is 3D assets.** Engineering is ~8–11 weeks; producing 20–50 sellable shoe models is a parallel content programme that starts in week 1, not after the code lands.
- **Effort units:** Small < 3 days · Medium 3–7 days · Large 1.5–3 weeks · Very Large 3+ weeks.
- **Gate before every phase close:** `flutter analyze lib test && flutter test` passes, plus the phase's own exit criteria.

## 1. North star & success metrics

> **A customer in Carcar or Cebu City can see a specific pair of artisan shoes on their own foot, believes the size recommendation, and adds to cart with confidence — without a return reason of "doesn't fit".**

| Metric | Baseline (Sept 2026) | Target after V5 | How measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| Catalog coverage of 3D models | 0 products | ≥ 20 hero products, ≥ 60% of product-detail traffic reaches a modelled product | `product_models` rows with `status='active'`; detail-view telemetry |
| Try-on sessions | 0 | ≥ 25% of modelled-product detail views open the try-on | `try_on_events` `session_start` |
| Try-on → add to cart | — | ≥ 30% of try-on sessions add to cart in-session | `session_start` → `add_to_cart` funnel |
| Model load p95 (warm cache) | — | ≤ 1.5 s | `modelLoaded.loadMs` |
| Render fps p50 / p95 | — | ≥ 45 / ≥ 30 on mid devices | `perf` events |
| Foot lock success in good light | — | ≥ 85% of sessions reach `footLock` | `foot_locked` events |
| Fit-verdict coverage | — | ≥ 50% of modelled products have fit specs filled | `products.last_length_mm IS NOT NULL` |
| Size-related return/ complaint rate | current (measure first!) | **measure at V1, improve by V5** | seller complaints + review text mining |
| Crash-free sessions during try-on | — | ≥ 99.5% | Sentry (already integrated) |

Honesty rule: **V1 must record a baseline** for size-related complaints/returns. Without it, the feature's business claim can never be evaluated.

## 2. Constraints & principles

| Constraint | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Android-first (ARCore only) | All native work in `android/app/src/main/kotlin/com/solevision/app/artryon/`; no iOS work packages in v1 |
| No third-party try-on SDK (decision D1) | We own the renderer integration; no per-MAU cost; camera frames stay on device |
| The shipped scan flow must not regress | New platform view `ar_try_on` in a new package; `arfoot/*` frozen; its tests stay the tripwire |
| Model assets are the bottleneck | Content pipeline (partners, tooling, validation) runs in parallel from V0 |
| Every phase degrades gracefully | No model / unsupported device / failed load ⇒ existing simulated screen, never a dead end |
| Sellers are artisans, not 3D artists | Upload UX must validate loudly and explain "last length" plainly; expect a support burden |

The design points worth reading the code for:
>
> 1. **A link, not a picker — and that is a gap, not a preference.** `image_picker` cannot select a `.glb` and no general file picker is a dependency of this project (verified: `file_picker` is not even transitive), so the handover arrives as a URL the studio shares and `DioShoeModelBytesSource` fetches it — with the byte cap enforced **mid-download** (`CancelToken`), because a wrong link (a Drive preview page) can be arbitrarily large and buffering it whole would be a memory failure on the cheapest phones in this market. The link must be https, except loopback so a developer can stage a file locally. Adding a picker later is a second source feeding the same `ShoeModelAsset`.
> 2. **Bytes before the row, always.** A `product_models` row whose object is missing renders as "no model" at best and as a failed download on every launch at worst, while an object with no row is simply invisible — the same asymmetry V2.5's `.part`-then-rename write protects on the read side, so the upload writes storage first and the table second (and a test asserts that order).
> 3. **Re-publishing a file is a no-op.** The digest is the storage filename, so `shoeModelReuseMatch` finds the existing row and flips its status instead of writing a duplicate — without that rule, "publish this model again" would create a second row pointing at one object and give the resolver two candidates for one asset.
> 4. **A failed check is a report, not an exception.** The seller has to read *which* rows failed, so the check returns a report either way; only a transport problem (bad scheme, 404, private link, unreachable host) throws, and each cause has its own sentence.
> 5. **A model failure never rolls back the product.** The upload runs after the product row is saved and reports itself in the snackbar ("Product saved, but the 3D model did not upload…") — losing the seller's product to a bad asset would be the D8 mistake in reverse.
>
> **Three deferrals, each because a required input does not exist yet.** *(a) The file picker* — no dependency can select a `.glb`, so the link is what ships. *(b) The per-colour override* (`variant_id`) — the app's variants are colour **and** size (`{'color': 'Black', 'size': 'EU 42'}`), so attaching a model to one variant id would show it on exactly one size: the resolver's exact-match rule would faithfully honour a row that means something the seller never intended. A per-colour model needs either a colour column or a resolution rule by colour, and that is its own decision (D-2). *(c) The material map* — `product_models.material_map` stores `{part: {baseColorHex, metallic, roughness}}`, but the product's colours are free-text **names** with no paint value (`product_color_images` carries only a photo URL), and the repo's single name→colour map (`lib/utils/variant_swatch_color.dart`) is a **UI swatch** whose unknown-name branch is a deterministic *synthetic* fallback. Adopting it as albedo would paint a shoe a colour nobody chose — the same mistake the fit card refuses to make by hiding a verdict it cannot support. > **Why the section was off, and why it now ships on (2026-09-28).** `shoeModelUploadEnabled` used to default to `false`, with `--dart-define=SHOE_MODEL_UPLOAD=true` to opt in, on the reasoning that V2.1's table and bucket were unwritten: a shipped build's Upload button could then only ever produce "relation does not exist", and a broken button is worse than an absent section. **That reasoning was sound and its premise was false** — the table had already been applied and never recorded, so the switch was waiting on nothing. It now defaults to `true`; `--dart-define=SHOE_MODEL_UPLOAD=false` restores exactly the previous day's form, and the kill switch is read by the save path as well as the section, so turning it off cannot leave a half-published model. **The caveat that outlives the flip:** the pipeline is now *reachable*, not *fed* — `product_models` holds 0 rows until a partner hands over a real `.glb`, so the section is live for every seller and there is nothing to show it yet.

**Exit criteria:** 3 **real pairs** have been captured with their partners, cleaned, and have validated active models that download and cache on device — declaration sheet and validator output attached to each; a deliberately broken GLB is rejected both client- and server-side; cache eviction proven under the 50 MB budget.
**Rollback:** assets are additive; disable by leaving `status='draft'`.

>
> **Four things this entry pinned, each because the alternative was a bug on a device.** *(1) What a phase means:* a phase describes the **AR session**, a mode describes the **surface** — so a product with no model is `idle` with a stated reason, not an error, and V5's error dashboards will not fill up with "this pair has no 3D model". *(2) The flag gates side effects (F19):* with `tryOnV3Enabled` off, the controller reads nothing, downloads nothing and makes **zero channel calls**, which is tested rather than asserted in prose. *(3) The handover precedes the view (F18):* the model is handed to native the moment it is verified, because the plugin parks it — the V0 plugin applied it through `currentView?.` and silently dropped it, which is the bug this ordering exists to not repeat. *(4) The start refuses without a view (F17):* `startAr({required bool arViewReady})` will not call `startSession` unless its caller says the platform view is mounted, so the documented 15-second deadlock becomes an inert fallback with `TryOnDegradeReason.arViewNotReady`.
