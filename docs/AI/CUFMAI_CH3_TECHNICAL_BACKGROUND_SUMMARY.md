# Summary of Chapter 3: Technical Background  
**Existing Manual System Analysis**  

## 1. Organizational Structure  
- **Partner Store:** Small footwear retail business in Valladolid, Carcar City, Cebu, Philippines.  
- **Structure:** Single-person, centralized operation managed solely by the owner.  
- **Roles Performed by Owner:**  
  - Customer service and sales processing  
  - Inventory management and stock tracking  
  - Record-keeping and basic reporting  
  - Handling customization requests  
- **Limitations:**  
  - No formal organizational chart or employee hierarchy.  
  - Owner handles all responsibilities, leading to potential inefficiencies during peak hours.  
  - Direct customer interaction but limited capacity for multitasking.  

## 2. Current Manual Workflow  
The existing system relies on manual processes for core retail operations:  
1. **Inventory Management:** Physical stock checks, handwritten logs, and manual updates. Prone to errors, delays, and stock discrepancies.  
2. **Sales Processing:** Manual recording of transactions (cash/GCash), receipt generation (handwritten or basic printed), and end-of-day tallying.  
3. **Customer Interaction:** In-store browsing, verbal inquiries about sizes/designs, and manual order taking. No virtual fitting or digital customization options.  
4. **Order Tracking:** Paper-based or simple spreadsheets for tracking custom orders, leading to potential miscommunication and delays.  

## 3. Key Inefficiencies Identified  
- **Performance:** Slow transaction processing and decision-making due to manual steps.  
- **Information:** Disorganized records (paper-based) prone to loss, damage, and unauthorized access.  
- **Economy:** Resource wastage (time, labor) and potential revenue loss from stock errors or missed sales.  
- **Control/Security:** Lack of secure data storage; manual records vulnerable to tampering or loss.  
- **Efficiency:** Owner spends excessive time on administrative tasks rather than customer engagement or business growth.  
- **Services:** Customers lack modern conveniences (virtual fitting, digital customization) offered by competitors.  

## 4. Problems Addressed by OTOP  
| Problem | Cause | Effect | OTOP Solution |  
|---------|-------|--------|---------------|  
| Size selection difficulties | No virtual fitting assistance | Customer uncertainty, returns, dissatisfaction | AR-based virtual fitting via smartphone |  
| Limited product visualization | Static displays, no interactive tools | Poor engagement, incorrect selections | Augmented reality features for immersive viewing |  
| Lack of customization options | No digital personalization features | Reduced satisfaction, unmet preferences | Customization module (color, style, design) |  
| High risk of incorrect selection | Inaccurate product visualization | Expectation vs. actual product mismatch | Enhanced visualization through AR preview |  
| Time-consuming decision-making | Trial-and-error process | Delayed purchases, lost sales | AR preview and filter tools for efficient selection |  

## 5. System Design Framework  
- **Hierarchical Input-Process-Output (HIPO):** Maps system functions from inputs (store owner data, customer browsing) to processes (inventory updates, fitting simulations) to outputs (processed orders, fitting results).  
- **Entity Relationship Diagram (ERD):** Defines entities (Customer, Seller, Admin, Product, Order, Customization) and their relationships.  
- **Use Case Diagrams:** Illustrate interactions for three user roles:  
  - **Customer:** Browse products, virtual fitting, customization, checkout.  
  - **Seller:** Manage products/inventory, process orders/POS, view reports.  
  - **Admin:** User management, seller approvals, system monitoring.  
- **Flowcharts:** Detailed process flows for each module (Customer Dashboard, Product Detail, Virtual Fitting, Customization, Order Placement, Tracking, Notifications, Cart, Checkout, Sales Reports, Inventory Management, Order Management, POS).  

## 6. Implications for Survey Questionnaire  
The existing manual system's limitations highlight the need for assessing:  
1. **User satisfaction** with current manual processes vs. proposed digital solutions.  
2. **Perceived usefulness** of automation (inventory, sales tracking, customization).  
3. **Usability expectations** for virtual fitting and AR features.  
4. **Readiness for change** among store owner and customers.
