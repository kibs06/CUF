## Unreleased — the product card's photo can be swiped

- **A customer can preview a product's other photos without leaving the feed.** The card's photo area pages sideways: one page per image, in the order the seller arranged them, with a row of dots at the bottom of the photo saying how many there are and which one is showing (`the current one is widened into a pill`). One photo means no dots and nothing to swipe, so the single-image card is exactly what it was.
- **It is a preview, not a second tap target.** A horizontal drag pages the photos; a tap on the photo still reaches the card and opens the product, and a vertical drag still scrolls the feed — the pager is a `PageView`, which answers drags only. `product_card_image_swipe_test.dart` pins all three at the card level (a swipe reports no tap, a tap reports exactly one).
- **The dots and the sale countdown share the bottom edge rather than fighting over it.** The countdown band is handed to the pager as its `bottomOverlay`, and the pager stacks them: the dots sit *above* the band when a sale has an end date, and on the photo's own bottom edge when it does not — no magic number for the band's height and no chance of the dots landing on the countdown's text.
- **One extractor, not a fourth copy of the same four lines.** `lib/utils/product_images.dart` reads a product's photos from either shape the map arrives in (a raw row's `product_images`, sorted by `display_order`, or the mapped model's flat `images`) and de-duplicates blanks and repeats. The card's pager is its first consumer.
- **A recycled grid cell cannot open on the wrong photo.** The grids rebuild cells in place, so a pager can be handed a different product mid-swipe; it watches its own image list and resets to the first photo when it changes.
- Tests: new `product_images_test.dart` (order, both shapes, blanks/repeats, the unmodifiable result) and `product_image_pager_test.dart` (no dots on one photo, a dot per photo with the current one widened, a swipe paging and the dots following, a tap reaching the card underneath, the dots stacking above the band, a band with no dots on a single-photo product), plus `product_card_image_swipe_test.dart` for the card. `size_listing_screen_test.dart`'s scroll helper now names the page's own scroll view — every card carries a scrollable photo pager, so `find.byType(Scrollable)` is no longer the page alone. `flutter analyze lib test` is clean and `flutter test` passes (1497).

## Unreleased — a product card sits down under the press

- **The cards answer a touch now.** `lib/widgets/press_sink.dart` wraps a card, watches the press and lerps its shadow from `productCardShadow` to the new `productCardShadowPressed` (110ms, ease out) — the ambient layer pulls in and drops, the contact layer tightens, so the tile reads as sitting *down* on the page for as long as the finger is on it, then rising back. Both product cards take it. No `InkWell`: these cards are `Container`s inside a plain `Stack`, and a ripple paints on the nearest `Material` ancestor — the page *behind* the card — so it would be invisible and the tap would look inert.

## Unreleased — the See more arrow moves on its own

- **The arrow invites now.** A looping idle beat — ~0.9s of stillness, a 550ms ease-out glide to the same 6%-of-width travel a press gets, a 450ms ease back — runs whether or not the customer is touching the card. The holds are the design: a constantly-drifting arrow next to products reads as noise, a beat reads as a nudge. Pressing still takes the arrow forward at once (the outer slide, 150ms) and **pauses the beat** while held, so the two motions never fight over the same pixels; releasing eases back to rest and restarts the beat from its hold.
- **The motor is frugal on purpose.** The beat is a `Timer`-scheduled `AnimationController`, not a repeating one: frames are only produced while the arrow is actually moving (~1s of every ~1.9s), the ticker lives **below** the card's `TickerMode` (so reduced motion mutes it for real — a ticker above the mode would keep running), and an opaque push over the card disposes it with the subtree. `see_more_card_test.dart` pins the envelope rather than FakeAsync frame timing: a scanned window measures how far out the arrow goes, that it comes back, that it loops, that a press freezes it at full travel, and that reduced motion never starts it.

## Unreleased — the grid's stagger is three buckets, all visibly different

- **`productGridRatio` dropped its fourth bucket.** The per-card image ratios were `[1.0, 0.78, 1.22, 0.95]`, keyed off the product id — but **0.95 is 1.0 to the eye**: 5% off square is ~12px on a 2-column card, and when a section's products all hashed into those two near-twin buckets the grid read as uniform rows (reported on "Based on your size", where all four products landed in exactly those two). ## Unreleased — The "Fit Card": "Based on your size" is a poster, not a heading

- **The words sit together now, and the reason is not a taste call.** The reference snippet pairs `height: 0.86` with a `TextHeightBehavior` that turns off first-ascent and last-descent padding "to remove the gaps". It does the opposite here: with those flags off, the height multiplier applies only to the space *between* lines, and each line is its own one-line `Text` — so `0.86` did nothing at all and every word rendered at the font's full ascent + descent (~1.27em on DM Sans). Because each line is also scaled to the card's full width, that gap was large: on device the three words filled the card, the hero `38` was squeezed to zero height and the card reported a **sub-pixel overflow** (`BOTTOM OVERFLOWED BY 0.454 PIXELS`). The default text-height behaviour is now used, which is what lets the leading bite — the words are ~35% tighter and the value has its height back. `fit_card_test.dart` pins this from the laid-out boxes rather than the source, so no font metric can hide it: the leading that comes out of a line is the one the widget declares (and is tighter than the font's own line box).
- **The size is scaled like the words.** The hero value now gets the exact treatment the copy above it gets — the same `fitWidth` line, scaled to the card's full width — so the number carries the card instead of sitting in a corner of it, and it is pinned to the card's bottom edge. The `EU` is the one thing that is *not* scaled to the width: it is a caption whose size is a fraction of the card's width (~14px on a 2-column cell, ~29px on a card twice as wide), instead of a fixed 18px that read huge on a small tile and got lost on a large one. Both are asserted (the value spans the card edge to edge; the caption's size scales with the card and stays a caption).
- **The card cannot overflow, and the value cannot vanish, whatever the copy or font does.** Nothing above the value is flexible — the words block sizes itself, the caption sits directly over the value, and any spare height is spent *between* the two blocks, so the number always lands on the bottom edge and no copy can squeeze it out. A cell *too short* for the content scales the whole poster down (`scaleDown`) instead of painting a striped overflow. Asserted, including on a deliberately absurd 200×90 cell.
