# SoleVision — Changelog

## Unreleased — the See more arrow moves on its own

- **The arrow invites now.** A looping idle beat — ~0.9s of stillness, a 550ms ease-out glide to the same 6%-of-width travel a press gets, a 450ms ease back — runs whether or not the customer is touching the card. The holds are the design: a constantly-drifting arrow next to products reads as noise, a beat reads as a nudge. Pressing still takes the arrow forward at once (the outer slide, 150ms) and **pauses the beat** while held, so the two motions never fight over the same pixels; releasing eases back to rest and restarts the beat from its hold.
- **The motor is frugal on purpose.** The beat is a `Timer`-scheduled `AnimationController`, not a repeating one: frames are only produced while the arrow is actually moving (~1s of every ~1.9s), the ticker lives **below** the card's `TickerMode` (so reduced motion mutes it for real — a ticker above the mode would keep running), and an opaque push over the card disposes it with the subtree. `see_more_card_test.dart` pins the envelope rather than FakeAsync frame timing: a scanned window measures how far out the arrow goes, that it comes back, that it loops, that a press freezes it at full travel, and that reduced motion never starts it.

## Unreleased — the grid's stagger is three buckets, all visibly different

- **`productGridRatio` dropped its fourth bucket.** The per-card image ratios were `[1.0, 0.78, 1.22, 0.95]`, keyed off the product id — but **0.95 is 1.0 to the eye**: 5% off square is ~12px on a 2-column card, and when a section's products all hashed into those two near-twin buckets the grid read as uniform rows (reported on "Based on your size", where all four products landed in exactly those two). Two buckets that look the same are not two buckets — the list is now `[1.0, 0.78, 1.22]`, each visibly distinct from the others. Card heights stay deterministic per product (same id → same ratio → same layout on every reload); existing products that hashed to 0.95 now sit in one of the three real buckets, which can shift which column a card is tallest in but not the shared geometry (gutter, margin, width) or any card's identity across surfaces — the ratio is the same function everywhere it is used.

## Unreleased — The home shelf previews ten, then hands over to a "See more" card

- **The cap is the section's now, and it comes with a way out.** `kHomePreviewCount` = 10 sits in `in_your_size_section.dart` (never a magic number): the tile is a cell too, so 1 + 10 + 1 = 12 cells — six clean rows on the two-column feed — and the eleventh+ match is reached through a card instead of a longer feed. `ProductProvider.productsInSize` defaulted to a rail-sized sample before; it now returns the **whole** shelf (`limit: 0`, the convention `productsInAudience`/`productsInSearch` already follow), because the section is the only thing that knows how long a preview should be — and a pre-truncated list could not tell it whether "See more" was honest. A personal shelf that grew without limit would be a second catalog above the real one.
- **"See more" is the size poster with different copy, not a new component.** `lib/widgets/see_more_card.dart` is a `FitCard` — same fill, edge, radius, DM Sans, same per-line scaling — saying `See` / `more` over an arrow. It renders **whenever the section does**, unconditionally: on a shelf longer than the preview it is the way to the rest of it, and on a short one (the live catalog today) it is still the door to the shelf as a place — a page that exists while the feed scrolls on and grows as sellers add stock. Hiding it at ≤10 products was the original rule, and on this catalog it would hide the only way in. *(Amended the same day after checking the live catalog: the shelf held ten or fewer, so the card never appeared.)* It is **pinned to the bottom-RIGHT** of the grid, one cell wide on the reference proportion, **beside the last product** — and pinned is the word: neither it nor the product above it is a cell of the masonry flow, because that flow packs every cell into whichever column is currently shorter, which on a shelf whose left column runs short left the whole closing row ragged (card left under the short column; then, after pinning it right, a lone product right with nothing beside it). Instead the LAST product comes out of the flow to join the card, and the section closes on one fixed row — product left, card right, one cell wide each, seamed by the same gutter every grid row uses. The flow above keeps the masonry look; the ending is the one row this design wants deterministic. Still absent with the whole section: empty catalog, no size on file, kill switch off.
- **The arrow is painted, so it scales like the type.** `ArrowGlyph` is a `CustomPaint` mark (no package, no font glyph, none of the fallback-font variance a `→` has) at a fixed 140×100 that the card scales as a block, left-aligned on the bottom edge the hero value would sit on, in the clay *ink* role. On press it nudges 6% of its own width, `easeOut`, 150ms, and returns — skipped when the platform asks for reduced motion (`MediaQuery.disableAnimations`, the convention the sale tags already follow) while the ripple and the action stay. `FitCard` grew a `heroWidget` for it (`heroValue` and `heroWidget` are mutually exclusive, asserted) plus `semanticsLabel`/`onHighlightChanged`, because paint contributes no words: the card announces itself as "See more products" and the whole card is the target.
- **The destination is a page, and it is the same shelf.** `lib/screens/customer/size_listing_screen.dart` shows the whole shelf, uncapped, in the preview's own ranking, with the same `SoleProductCard` and the same per-product ratio — so a product looks identical on both sides of the arrow — headed by the size it was built on and its count. It deliberately has **no sort control**: the section that opens it has none either, so the list a customer tapped is the list they get. `MasonryGridView` (lazy) rather than the feed's `shrinkWrap` grid, since this page owns its own scroll. It is absent-safe like every other size surface (no size on file, or nothing in that size, renders an explanation with a way back instead of a guessed size), and it reads the catalog already in memory — no query, no network.
- Tests: `see_more_card_test.dart` (the mark's proportion/alignment, the nudge and its return, reduced motion, the semantics label and tap target, the accent ink in both brightnesses), `size_listing_screen_test.dart` (uncapped and reachable to its last card, the same card and ratio, no invented sort control, both empty states, and that its way out really pops) and the section's own suite (ten shown with the eleventh behind the arrow, exactly ten with no arrow, the arrow as the grid's last one-cell card, and the tap landing on `SizeListingScreen`). `flutter analyze lib test` is clean and `flutter test` passes.

## Unreleased — The "Fit Card": "Based on your size" is a poster, not a heading

- **The words sit together now, and the reason is not a taste call.** The reference snippet pairs `height: 0.86` with a `TextHeightBehavior` that turns off first-ascent and last-descent padding "to remove the gaps". It does the opposite here: with those flags off, the height multiplier applies only to the space *between* lines, and each line is its own one-line `Text` — so `0.86` did nothing at all and every word rendered at the font's full ascent + descent (~1.27em on DM Sans). Because each line is also scaled to the card's full width, that gap was large: on device the three words filled the card, the hero `38` was squeezed to zero height and the card reported a **sub-pixel overflow** (`BOTTOM OVERFLOWED BY 0.454 PIXELS`). The default text-height behaviour is now used, which is what lets the leading bite — the words are ~35% tighter and the value has its height back. `fit_card_test.dart` pins this from the laid-out boxes rather than the source, so no font metric can hide it: the leading that comes out of a line is the one the widget declares (and is tighter than the font's own line box).
- **The size is scaled like the words.** The hero value now gets the exact treatment the copy above it gets — the same `fitWidth` line, scaled to the card's full width — so the number carries the card instead of sitting in a corner of it, and it is pinned to the card's bottom edge. The `EU` is the one thing that is *not* scaled to the width: it is a caption whose size is a fraction of the card's width (~14px on a 2-column cell, ~29px on a card twice as wide), instead of a fixed 18px that read huge on a small tile and got lost on a large one. Both are asserted (the value spans the card edge to edge; the caption's size scales with the card and stays a caption).
- **The card cannot overflow, and the value cannot vanish, whatever the copy or font does.** Nothing above the value is flexible — the words block sizes itself, the caption sits directly over the value, and any spare height is spent *between* the two blocks, so the number always lands on the bottom edge and no copy can squeeze it out. A cell *too short* for the content scales the whole poster down (`scaleDown`) instead of painting a striped overflow. Asserted, including on a deliberately absurd 200×90 cell.

- **`FitCard` is the pattern now.** A reusable category tile whose typography *is* the design: 2–3 short lines, each laid out at a 100px reference size and scaled by a `FittedBox` to exactly the card's inner width (so a short word renders bigger, every line touches both edges, and nothing here hardcodes a font size), an optional small accent label, and a hero value filling the height left under them. `AspectRatio(aspectRatio: FitCard.aspectRatio)` — the reference 505×800, about a product card's footprint — is how a self-sizing parent bounds it, which the widget documents rather than guessing a height of its own.
- **The section's heading is that card.** `ProductGridSection` now takes *either* a `title` or a `tile` (mutually exclusive, asserted). "Based on your size" passes a `FitCard`, which takes the grid's **first cell** — top-left, one full cell wide, the first product in the row's other cell — so the size poster is part of the shelf instead of a line of text above it. The size it reports is the card's hero value (`EU` over `42`, via the new `euSizeValue`), replacing the muted 12px meta that used to carry it, and the tile is not tappable yet (`onTap` null): no button semantics, no ripple, no destination it does not have.
- **The mockup's warm palette was mapped onto the app's tokens, not pasted.** The reference specifies `#F5F1EC` fill with `#9A5B2E` accent, which predates the neutral palette — and the app paints from brightness-aware roles, so hardcoded hexes would have been wrong on dark. The card's defaults are fill `surfaceSubtle`, edge `hairline`, ink `secondary`, accent `AppPalette.primaryInk` (the clay *ink* role, lifted on dark), radius `cardRadius` (the 28 deferred to product-card consistency); every one is overridable per card if a surface wants the warmer treatment. Measured, not assumed: the ink/fill and accent/fill pairs clear **AA (≥4.5:1) in both brightnesses** in the card's own test.
- **Verified rather than eyeballed.** `test/widgets/fit_card_test.dart` pins the behaviour that makes the pattern work — every line exactly the inner width, the hero flush with the bottom padding, adjacent lines with no gap between them, a 2.0× OS text scale producing byte-identical boxes (the card scales with its cell), `42.5` fitting without overflow, and 120/168/320/430px widths laying out clean — plus one combined semantics label instead of five fragments. `google_fonts` was already a dependency and DM Sans was already the app's body face, so the card adds no package.

## Unreleased — Seller performance: instant tab switching, one catalog fetch, and the indexes the dashboard was missing

- **Switching tabs no longer reloads the screen.** `SellerShell` hosts its five pages in a `PageView`, whose viewport builds only the page it is showing — so scrolling away *disposed* the previous page, and coming back ran `initState()` again: that screen refetched its data and re-showed its skeleton. Every page is now wrapped in `KeepAlivePage`, so a visited tab stays mounted and switching to it toggles visibility instead of rebuilding. `CustomerShell` gets the same treatment. Proven, not assumed: a control test asserts that a bare `PageView` child's `initState` fires **twice** across one switch-away-and-back, and the wrapped one fires once.
- **A kept-alive page had no way to notice it was visible again — so the TTL refresh logic that already existed was dead code.** With the page no longer rebuilt, `build` does not re-run on re-entry either, which removes the only moment a screen could see it had come back. `ProfileScreen` already carried three refresh-on-re-entry blocks documented as "the screen lives in an `IndexedStack`, so re-entry re-runs build but not `initState`" — true for the admin shell, false for the two shells that actually needed it. New `ActiveTab` publishes the on-screen tab index and screens hook it from `didChangeDependencies`. Re-entry refresh is **opt-in and TTL-gated** (60s); re-selecting a tab is never on its own a reason to hit the network.
- **One catalog, not three.** Dashboard, POS and Products each fetched the seller's own products, through three different queries (one of them a heavier 5-join variant that bypassed the provider entirely). `ProductProvider` now owns a single shared catalog that all three read. Its load is cache-aware: a hit returns **without a store lookup, without a catalog query, and deliberately without `notifyListeners()`**, so a tab that already has the data cannot even repaint, let alone flash a skeleton. A refresh never clears the rows it already has, the cache is keyed on user + store (an app-root singleton must not answer a previous account's read), and concurrent callers share one in-flight request. The grid's in-place edits moved to the provider with it.
- **The admin shell stopped building all six tabs at login.** It used a bare `IndexedStack`, which keeps every child it is handed mounted — so all six admin screens ran their `initState` (and their fetches) on the first frame, competing with the screen the admin was actually looking at. New `LazyIndexedStack` (`lib/widgets/lazy_indexed_stack.dart`) keeps the state-preservation guarantee while rendering a slot only after its index has been visited once. Index drift is guarded by a contract test, since nothing in the language ties a screen's hardcoded tab index to its position in the shell's list.
- **Two timers that used to die with their page now stop when their tab is off screen.** The Products tab's 4-second alert rotation and the dashboard's 30-second GCash payments-to-confirm sweep both live for the whole session now that their page is kept alive; leaving them running meant rebuilding an invisible page and polling the backend while the seller worked elsewhere. Each pauses on the way out and resumes on the way back in — and the pause *clears* its field, not just cancels, because both `_start*` methods bail out early on a non-null timer (so a cancel-only pause would make every later resume a silent no-op). The GCash card sweeps once immediately on return, so its count is never more than a frame stale.
- **The migration the dashboard was missing:** `20260918200000_add_hot_query_indexes.sql` adds 11 foreign-key indexes across `orders`, `order_items`, `products`, `product_images`, `product_variants` and `product_customizations`. The critical one is `orders(store_id, created_at DESC)` — `fetchOrders()` applies **no `store_id` filter at all**, so the entire orders table was sequentially scanned on every dashboard load with the "sellers can view orders for their store" RLS policy re-evaluated per row.
